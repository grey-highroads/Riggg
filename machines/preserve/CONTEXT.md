# The Memory Vault — Context

## Feature Identity

**Machine name:** The Memory Vault
**RIGGG feature:** Preserve
**Accent color:** `accent-amber` (#FF9800)
**Assigned gnome:** Keeper

## Business Context

**What this feature does (plain language):**
Preserve makes content compound. It covers transcripts, searchable archives, vector memory, content libraries, theme extraction, reuse patterns, and AI/search visibility. The goal is that a teammate can ask a natural-language question and retrieve the relevant prior moments with source context. Nothing the organization has said or published should be lost, unfindable, or unreusable.

**The problem it solves:**
Owned media should not die in a folder after the recording. Most organizations produce sessions, publish them once, and never touch them again. The knowledge, quotes, stories, data points, and expertise expressed in those sessions become inaccessible. Teams re-record topics they've already covered. Sales can't find the clip that would close the deal. Content writers don't know what the company has already said about a topic. The content library exists but can't be searched, retrieved, or reused.

**The outcome it delivers:**
A searchable, vector-indexed content memory. Clean transcripts with speaker labels and timestamps. Session, program, and guest metadata. Topic tags and theme maps. Vector embeddings that enable natural-language retrieval. Structured show notes optimized for LLM discovery. Schema markup and citation architecture for AI search (AEO). A reuse and repurposing library that makes prior sessions useful for callbacks, clips, quotes, theme analysis, sales enablement, and future publishing.

**Where it sits in the workflow:**
Fifth and final in the chain. Produce → Package → Publish → Prove → **Preserve**. But also the stage that feeds back into all others — preserved content becomes source material for future production, packaging, and publishing. This is where the system becomes a flywheel.

**Quality question (from the framework):**
"Can the organization find and reuse what it already knows?"

## What Gets Made Here (Owned Assets)

These are the longest-lived owned assets. They compound over time.

## Emotional Register

**How this feature should feel:**
Protective and wise. The Memory Vault is the library and archive — the institutional memory of the entire operation. There's warmth in the preservation act, a sense of stewardship. The Vault doesn't just store things; it organizes them with care so they can be found and used again. It's the stage that turns a media operation into a knowledge operation.

**Tone for copy about this feature:**
Warm and steady. "Everything you've said. Findable. Reusable. Compounding."

## Metaphor Mapping

**Physical metaphor:** A vault/archive with glass-fronted filing cabinets, organized card catalog drawers, and a retrieval mechanism. Content cards are carefully filed, indexed, and stored in warm amber-lit shelves. A mechanical retrieval arm can locate and present any prior session. The machine has the most visible storage and organization elements of any in the factory.

**Input → Output:**
- **What goes in:** Published and proven content from all prior stages
- **What the machine does:** Transcribes, indexes, tags, embeds (vectors), organizes, and archives
- **What comes out:** Retrievable knowledge — any prior moment accessible by natural-language query, with source context

**Why this metaphor works:**
Preservation is archiving — carefully storing things so they last and can be found. The vault/library metaphor captures both the protective instinct (nothing gets lost) and the retrieval capability (anything can be found). The amber glow suggests warmth, age, and accumulated value — a library lit by lamplight, not a cold data warehouse.
