# The Distribution Engine — Context

## Feature Identity

**Machine name:** The Distribution Engine
**RIGGG feature:** Publish
**Accent color:** `accent-blue` (#2196F3)
**Assigned gnome:** Router

## Business Context

**What this feature does (plain language):**
Publish gets packaged assets into the right places. It manages the distinction between owned channels (website, email list, RSS feed, podcast hosting, owned community) and shared distribution (YouTube, Spotify, Apple Podcasts, LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Facebook). The critical principle: you own what you create, you do not own the pipe you send it through.

**The problem it solves:**
Teams publish to one or two channels and call it done. They skip the canonical episode page, don't optimize RSS metadata, post to social without platform-specific formatting, and never set up simulcast or live premiere workflows. The content exists but doesn't reach the channels and audiences it was created for. Platform distribution is rented attention — without a canonical owned home, the content has no durable address.

**The outcome it delivers:**
Complete distribution across owned and shared channels. Website-first canonical publishing with embedded players, transcripts, schema markup, and CTAs. Optimized RSS feeds. YouTube with proper titles, descriptions, and chapters. LinkedIn release posts with proven patterns. Email distribution. Live premiere and simulcast workflows. Every asset reaches every relevant channel with platform-appropriate formatting.

**Where it sits in the workflow:**
Third in the chain. Produce → Package → **Publish** → Prove → Preserve. Takes packaged assets and distributes them across the full channel landscape.

**Quality question (from the framework):**
"Did the media actually reach the channels and audiences it was created for?"

## What Gets Made Here

**Owned channels:** Canonical episode pages (website), email releases, RSS feed entries, podcast hosting configuration, owned community posts
**Shared distribution:** YouTube uploads, Spotify/Apple Podcasts episodes, LinkedIn posts, Instagram reels/stories, X/Twitter posts, Facebook posts, live simulcasts
**AI search optimization:** Citation architecture, structured data/schema markup, show notes optimized for LLMs, transcript optimization, platform visibility configuration

## Key Patterns (from the Index)

- Canonical Episode Page (Riggg Score: 5) — the owned home for every session
- Feed Metadata Standard — RSS feed structure and optimization
- YouTube Title and Description Standard
- LinkedIn Release Post Patterns
- Simulcast Standard — live premiere across multiple platforms
- AI Search Optimization suite: citation architecture, structured data, show notes for LLMs, transcript optimization, platform visibility, measurement

## Metaphor Mapping

**Physical metaphor:** A sorting and routing station with multiple labeled output chutes. Content enters from one side and is directed to specific channel destinations through a mechanical sorting mechanism. Each output chute is labeled (Website, RSS, YouTube, LinkedIn, Email) and has its own indicator light.

**Input → Output:**
- **What goes in:** Packaged assets from the Assembly Press
- **What the machine does:** Sorts, formats, routes, and dispatches to each channel
- **What comes out:** Platform-ready assets delivered to labeled channel destinations

**Why this metaphor works:**
Publishing is distribution — physically moving things to their destinations. The sorting/routing metaphor makes the complexity visible (many channels, each with its own requirements) while showing the system handling it with mechanical precision.
