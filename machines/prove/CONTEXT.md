# The Insight Scope — Context

## Business Context

**What this feature does (plain language):**
Prove measures whether the owned media system worked. It covers reach, engagement, watch/listen behavior, conversion events, CTA performance, CRM attribution, pipeline influence, benchmarks, and scorecards. The point isn't data collection — it's helping the team see what shipped, what worked, and what should change next.

**The problem it solves:**
Owned media performance is not one metric. A session can succeed through reach, engagement, conversion, reuse, sales enablement, or long-term search value — but most teams either don't measure at all or track vanity metrics that don't connect to business outcomes. Without a scorecard that spans production quality, packaging completeness, publishing coverage, and business impact, teams can't tell what to repeat, improve, or stop doing.

**The outcome it delivers:**
Session performance scorecards that evaluate each session across production quality, packaging completeness, publishing completion, reach, engagement, clicks, conversion events, sales/customer usefulness, reuse potential, and library value. Benchmark comparisons across sessions and programs. CRM attribution data connecting content to pipeline. Clear reporting that drives operational decisions.

**Where it sits in the workflow:**
Fourth in the chain. Produce → Package → Publish → **Prove** → Preserve. Evaluates what happened after publishing to inform future decisions.

**Quality question (from the framework):**
"Can the team see what shipped, what worked, and what should change next?"

## Scoring System Context

The Index itself uses a 1-5 scoring system for patterns:
- 5 = Recommended Default
- 4 = Strong Fit
- 3 = Viable
- 2 = Niche
- 1 = Not Recommended

Each score is backed by an evidence level: hypothesis, practitioner observation, internal data, external research, or benchmark-backed.

## Emotional Register

**How this feature should feel:**
Observant and analytical. The Insight Scope isn't cold or clinical — it's a thoughtful scientist who finds meaning in patterns and communicates findings clearly. There's satisfaction in seeing what worked and intellectual curiosity about why. It doesn't judge; it illuminates.

Clear and grounded. "See what worked. Understand why. Know what to do next."

## Metaphor Mapping

**Physical metaphor:** A precision measurement and observation apparatus — magnifying lenses, gauges, dials, and comparison scales. Content cards pass under analysis instruments, get weighed/measured, and receive scores that sort into performance tiers. The machine has the most visible gauges and dials of any in the factory.

**Input → Output:**
- **What goes in:** Published content performance data (signals flowing back from distributed assets)
- **What the machine does:** Measures, compares, scores, and visualizes performance
- **What comes out:** Scorecards, benchmark reports, and attribution data — rendered as printed report cards and filed analysis sheets

**Why this metaphor works:**
Proving is measurement — examining things closely, comparing against standards, and reporting what you find. The magnifying/measuring metaphor makes the analytical process physical while keeping it warm (a craftsperson inspecting their work, not a cold algorithm).
