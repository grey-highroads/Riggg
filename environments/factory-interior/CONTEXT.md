# Factory Interior — Context

## Identity

**Name:** The Factory Floor
**Role:** The internal working environment — where the machines operate and the gnomes do their work. If the exterior is the "establishing shot," the interior is the "action shot."

## Business Context

**What it represents:**
The factory floor is the RIGGG system in motion. Multiple machines visible, conveyor belts connecting them, gnomes at their stations. It shows the pipeline as a living, connected workflow rather than isolated features.

**When to use it:**
- "How it works" sections that show the full pipeline
- Process illustrations where the connection between stages matters
- Overview content where you need to show the system, not a single feature
- Background context for team/culture content
- Video or animation backgrounds

**When NOT to use it:**
- Individual feature pages (use the specific machine instead)
- Contexts where a single capability is the focus
- Small-format placements where interior detail won't read

## Narrative Role

The interior is the "working shot" — it shows the factory in operation. Where the exterior says "this is the place," the interior says "this is what happens inside." The warm amber lighting, the visible conveyor connections between machines, and the gnomes at work all reinforce that RIGGG is a system, not a collection of disconnected tools.

## Key Visual Elements

**Central conveyor belt system:**
The spine of the factory floor. Connects all workstations. Content flows along it from left (Produce) to right (Preserve), with branching tracks at the Distribution Engine.

**Multiple workstations:**
Machines from the 5Ps visible along the conveyor. Not all need to be fully detailed — some can be background elements that establish scale and system.

**Warm amber overhead lighting:**
Industrial pendant lamps creating pools of warm light. This is the key differentiator from the exterior — the interior is warmer, more intimate, more workshop than factory.

**Gnomes at work:**
Multiple gnomes visible at their stations. This is the team in action.

**Environmental details:**
Tool boards on walls, potted plants on shelves, output trays between stations, worn wooden floor. These details make the space feel lived-in and real.
