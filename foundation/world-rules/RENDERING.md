# Rendering Rules — The RIGGG Factory World

These rules define how the RIGGG visual world looks. They are non-negotiable across all illustration assets. Deviations require explicit approval and documentation of the reason.

## Style Classification

**Pixar-adjacent 3D illustration.** High-fidelity renders with subsurface scattering, volumetric lighting, and physically plausible materials — but with exaggerated proportions and a warm, toylike quality. This is not photorealism. It is not low-poly. It is not flat illustration. It is not concept art.

The closest reference points are Pixar's environmental design (the detail density of Monsters, Inc. factory floors) crossed with the material warmth of stop-motion (Laika's metalwork and wood textures).

## Camera & Perspective

- **Default angle:** Slightly elevated 3/4 isometric. Camera approximately 30° above horizontal, 30-45° rotated from front.
- **Framing:** Objects presented as "diorama on a stage." No ground plane extends beyond the object's base. Generous negative space around the subject.
- **Depth of field:** Subtle. Background elements slightly softer than foreground, but no aggressive bokeh. Everything should be readable.
- **No environmental context.** No sky, no horizon line, no room walls. Objects exist on the neutral cream background.

## Lighting

- **Key light:** Warm white, upper-left. Creates clear shadow direction across all surfaces.
- **Fill light:** Cool-neutral, opposite side. Prevents harsh shadows but maintains dimensionality.
- **Rim/accent light:** Subtle edge highlight on top surfaces, creating separation from background.
- **Interior glow:** Translucent glass elements (jars, chambers, screens) emit their own light in the feature's accent color. This glow is volumetric — it illuminates nearby surfaces and casts colored light onto adjacent objects.
- **Ambient:** The cream background subtly reflects warm light back onto objects, preventing cold undersides.

## Materials

### Powder-coated metal (primary surfaces)
- Matte finish, very slight texture grain
- Primary color: teal-700 (#0A5858)
- Shows subtle wear at edges and contact points (not heavy weathering)
- Rivets and seam lines visible

### Aged bronze / brass (hardware and structure)
- Warm metallic with slight patina
- Used for: rivets, buckles, belt tracks, valve handles, frame elements, gauges
- Color range: bronze-600 (#705030) to bronze-400 (#907050)
- Reflects key light as warm highlights

### Translucent glass (processing chambers)
- Clear to slightly frosted
- Contains inner glow in the feature's accent color
- Light refracts and scatters through the glass
- Surface shows subtle reflections of surrounding elements

### Leather and fabric (gnome uniforms)
- Matte with visible weave/texture
- Slightly worn at stress points (elbows, knees)
- Belt leather shows grain and tooling marks

### Organic elements (plants, paper, wood)
- Small potted succulents and ferns as scale/warmth details
- Paper documents and cards as "media output" props
- Wooden drawer handles and small accessories
- These elements are always small and secondary — they humanize, not dominate

## Surface Detail Density

High detail on objects, clean composition overall. Every machine should have:
- At least 2-3 gauges or dials
- Visible rivets on structural joins
- Pipe or tube connections to adjacent elements
- A small organic detail (plant, paper stack, wooden element)

But: the overall frame should breathe. Detail fills objects, not negative space. The cream background is sacred — do not fill it with particles, gradients, or atmospheric effects.

## Scale

Gnomes are approximately 3 feet tall in-world. Machines are sized so a gnome can operate them while standing or sitting at a workstation. The factory building is multi-story. Use small organic elements (potted plants, stacked papers, tools) to reinforce consistent scale across assets.
